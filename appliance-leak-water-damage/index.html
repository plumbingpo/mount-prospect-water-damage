Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Mount Prospect

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Mount Prospect crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • A dishwasher with a failed pump seal, a cracked drain hose or a worn door gasket.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of rooms affected, counting a ceiling under a laundry on an upper floor.
  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
