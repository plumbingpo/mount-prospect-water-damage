Roof Leak Water Damage in Mount Prospect: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Hidden Attic Mold

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Drywall Ceilings

A ceiling panel left damp will discolor, bow and weaken. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.
