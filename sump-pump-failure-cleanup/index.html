Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Mount Prospect: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Carpet and Flooring

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.
  • The water category, which sets how much is discarded and disinfected.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • Water depth, along with the square footage that flooded.

Our Process

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
