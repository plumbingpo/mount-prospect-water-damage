A water damage claim is a lot to take on while your home is wet, torn apart, and out of its normal routine. Knowing how the claims process works, what is typically covered, and how to protect your claim can make a real difference in your financial recovery. This guide from Mount Prospect Restoration helps Mount Prospect, IL homeowners work through the insurance process with confidence.

What Policies Usually Cover

In general, a standard homeowner's policy pays for water damage when the event was accidental and happened suddenly. Typical examples are a burst pipe, an ice dam, a sink or bathtub that overflows by accident, a sudden HVAC failure, and a failed dishwasher, washing machine, or water heater. Coverage typically extends to water extraction, structural drying, material replacement, and related restoration costs, including temporary living expenses if your home is uninhabitable.

Damage That Is Often Excluded

Several kinds of loss are normally left out: groundwater seepage at the foundation, long-term leaks, problems caused by putting off maintenance, floods from external sources (a separate flood policy is needed), and sewer backups (a specific endorsement is needed). If you learn about the gaps ahead of time, you can close them with an endorsement or a separate policy.

Filing a Claim Step by Step

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Report the loss to your insurer as soon as you can. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has inspected them. Get professional restoration from an IICRC-certified company. Save every receipt tied to the loss, from temporary repairs to living expenses. Attend the inspection yourself and show the adjuster every damaged area.
